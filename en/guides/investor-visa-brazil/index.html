Brazil investor visa (VITEM IX): minimum investment, real estate option, documents and timeline

By Gustavo Russignoli Bugalho, lawyer (OAB/SP 235.825) · Updated October 2026 · 10 minute read

A foreign national can obtain Brazilian residence by investing at least R$500,000 of foreign capital in a Brazilian company, or at least R$150,000 in an innovation or research project, under a business plan with job or income potential. A separate route grants residence for buying urban real estate worth R$1,000,000, or R$700,000 in the North and Northeast. The residence for company investment is granted for an indefinite term but depends on actually executing the plan. The decision belongs to the Ministry of Justice; the visa itself, called VITEM IX, is issued afterwards by a Brazilian consulate, or the residence can be requested from inside Brazil. This guide covers the three routes, the documents, the Central Bank step most applicants get wrong, and what happens after approval.

The legal basis in one paragraph

Brazil's Migration Law of 2017 allows a temporary visa and a residence authorization for the purpose of making an investment in the country. The regulating decree says the residence may be granted to a foreign individual who intends to invest, or already invests, foreign funds of their own in a Brazilian legal entity in a project with potential to generate jobs or income, that it may be granted for an indefinite term, and that it can be revoked if the investment plan is not executed. The thresholds, documents and procedure are set by resolutions of the National Immigration Council, the most recent amendments dating from June 2024, when the rules were aligned with Brazil's new foreign-exchange framework and the Ministry of Justice became the deciding authority for all three routes.

Route 1: investing in a Brazilian company

This is the standard investor residence. The applicant, as an individual, invests foreign-sourced funds in a Brazilian company, new or existing, in which they hold shares. The general minimum is R$500,000. The sector does not matter, but the Ministry wants to see a plan: the application must include an investment or business plan describing the activity, the amount, the timeline and the expected jobs or income.

For entrepreneurs in innovation, basic or applied research of a scientific or technological nature, the Ministry may approve a smaller investment, no lower than R$150,000. This is the route used by technology founders, and the business plan carries more weight here because the money is smaller. The Ministry may also authorize residence on a lower amount when the receiving company meets conditions related to job creation set out in the resolution.

Two things define whether the investment qualifies. It must be foreign capital, proved by the foreign-exchange contract issued by a bank authorized by the Central Bank, with the operation coded as foreign direct investment. And it must be invested in the company, as capital, not lent to it or parked in a personal Brazilian account. Money that enters Brazil the wrong way can be real and still not count.

Route 2: buying real estate

A separate resolution grants residence to a foreign individual who buys urban real estate in Brazil, built or under construction, with foreign funds, for at least R$1,000,000. The threshold falls to R$700,000 when the property is in the North or Northeast regions. The amount can be spread across more than one property, and in the case of property under construction the registered promise-of-purchase agreement with the qualifying deposit may be used. Rural land does not qualify.

Unlike the company route, the real estate residence is granted initially for four years and can then be converted to an indefinite term if the conditions that justified it are maintained. Proof of the international transfer of funds, issued by an institution authorized by the Central Bank, is required, and the property must be registered in the applicant's name at the real estate registry. Some versions of the rule have required a minimum physical stay in Brazil during the initial period; the current wording should be checked at the time of filing.

Route 3: the executive sent by a foreign company

When the investor is a foreign company rather than a person, the residence is granted to the manager, director or executive it appoints to run the Brazilian business. The foreign company must invest at least R$600,000 per executive, or at least R$150,000 per executive combined with a commitment to create at least ten new jobs within two years. This route is used by groups opening a subsidiary and is tied to the executive's role: changing company or function requires the Ministry's consent, and the residence continues only while the job-creation commitment is proven.

Order of operations. The company must exist and the applicant must be a registered shareholder before the money comes in, so that the inbound transfer can be contracted as foreign direct investment into that company. Applicants who wire funds first and set up the structure afterwards often end up having to send the money out and bring it back in correctly, with the exchange costs that implies.

How the process works

For an applicant abroad, the sequence has three stages. First, a request for prior residence authorization is filed with the Ministry of Justice, with the documents above. Second, once the Ministry approves, the applicant submits the VITEM IX visa application at the Brazilian consulate with jurisdiction over their residence; consulates require the Ministry's authorization before they will accept the application. Third, after entering Brazil, the applicant registers with the Federal Police within ninety days and receives the residence card. Family members are included by family reunion once the principal applicant's residence is granted.

An applicant already in Brazil on a valid entry can skip the consular stage and ask the Ministry directly for the residence authorization, presenting the same documents. This is common for investors who first come as visitors to choose a city, meet partners and open the company.

How long it takes

The Ministry's analysis of a complete file is usually counted in weeks, not months, though it varies with demand and with requests for additional documents. Consulates publish their own processing times for the visa stage, often around two to three weeks. The slow part is before filing: incorporating the company, obtaining the applicant's Brazilian taxpayer number, opening the bank account that will receive the investment and contracting the exchange operation correctly. A realistic end-to-end horizon from decision to residence card is three to six months when the structure is done right the first time.

After approval: conditions, taxes and the road to citizenship

The company-investment residence is granted for an indefinite term, but it is not unconditional. The Ministry may require proof that the investment plan is being executed and may visit the company; failure to carry out the plan is a ground for loss of the residence. Investors should keep the company active, the capital registered with the Central Bank and the records that show the plan is real.

A resident investor becomes a Brazilian tax resident from the date of the permanent-type residence, which means worldwide income is taxed in Brazil, with relief depending on Brazil's domestic rules and on whether a tax treaty exists with the country of origin. The foreign investment itself must be registered in the Central Bank's electronic system; this registration is what allows dividends and the original capital to be remitted abroad later.

Residence obtained through investment counts toward naturalization. Ordinary naturalization requires four years of legal residence, the ability to communicate in Portuguese and no criminal conviction, reduced to one year for those with a Brazilian spouse or child. The investor route is, for many clients, the first step of a two-stage plan whose second stage is citizenship.

Common mistakes

Wiring the investment before the company and the shareholding exist. Bringing the money through a personal account instead of a foreign direct investment exchange contract. Presenting a generic business plan copied from a template, which the Ministry reads as a signal that the investment is a formality. Treating the real estate threshold as a per-property figure when it is cumulative, or buying rural or mixed-use property that does not qualify. Forgetting that the indefinite residence still depends on executing the plan. And, for executives, changing roles inside the group without telling the Ministry.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum investment for a Brazil investor visa?

R$500,000 of foreign capital in a Brazilian company, or at least R$150,000 for innovation or research projects. For real estate, R$1,000,000 in urban property, or R$700,000 in the North and Northeast.

Is the investor visa permanent?

The company-investment residence is granted for an indefinite term but is conditional on executing the plan. The real estate residence starts at four years and can be converted to an indefinite term.

Can I apply from inside Brazil?

Yes. The Ministry of Justice can grant the residence to an applicant already in Brazil on the same documents, without the consular visa stage.

Can my family come with me?

Yes. Spouse or partner, children and other dependents apply for residence by family reunion once the investor's residence is granted.

Does the investment have to stay in Brazil?

The capital must remain invested in the company, or the property kept, for the residence to be maintained. The Central Bank registration allows the capital and dividends to be remitted abroad later under the normal rules.

Does the investor visa lead to citizenship?

Residence under this route counts toward the four years required for ordinary naturalization, or one year with a Brazilian spouse or child.
