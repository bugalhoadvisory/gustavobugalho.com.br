Naturalization in Brazil: requirements, documents, timeline and how to apply

By Gustavo Russignoli Bugalho, lawyer (OAB/SP 235.825) · Updated October 2026 · 9 minute read

A foreigner can become a Brazilian citizen after four years of legal residence, or one year with a Brazilian spouse or child, provided they can communicate in Portuguese and have no criminal conviction. The application is filed online through the Ministry of Justice's Naturalizar-se platform, with an in-person appointment at the Federal Police for original documents and biometrics, and is decided by the Ministry of Justice. Brazil allows you to keep your original nationality. This guide explains each requirement, the documents to prepare, where the process usually stalls, and what changes once you are approved.

Who can apply: the four types of naturalization

Brazil's Migration Law of 2017 recognizes four routes. Which one applies to you depends mostly on how long you have lived in the country and on your family ties.

Ordinary naturalization

This is the route for most residents. You must be legally capable under Brazilian law, have resided in Brazil for at least four years with a valid residence authorization, be able to communicate in Portuguese, and have no criminal conviction in Brazil or abroad, unless rehabilitated. Residence means authorized residence; time spent on a tourist entry does not count, and gaps between permits can break the count. Occasional trips abroad do not interrupt the four-year count as long as their total does not exceed twelve months, and owning property in Brazil is not, by itself, proof of residence: the Ministry checks actual residence against official databases.

Ordinary naturalization with reduced residence

The four-year requirement drops to one year when the applicant has a Brazilian child, is married to or in a recognized stable union with a Brazilian citizen (with no legal separation at the time of filing), has rendered or is able to render relevant services to Brazil, or is recommended for professional, scientific or artistic capacity. The one-year reduction is the single most common reason a foreigner qualifies earlier than expected, and the most common mistake is assuming that a partner's citizenship alone is enough without the marriage or union being formally registered in Brazil.

Extraordinary naturalization

After fifteen years of uninterrupted residence, a foreigner with no criminal conviction may apply without the language requirement. The word uninterrupted matters: long absences from Brazil can be read as interruption, so the travel history of a fifteen-year resident is examined closely.

Provisional and special naturalization

Children who established residence in Brazil before the age of ten can obtain provisional naturalization, requested by their legal representative; it becomes definitive if the naturalized person asks for conversion within two years of reaching the age of majority. Special naturalization covers two narrow cases: foreign spouses of Brazilian diplomats married for more than five years, and foreigners who have worked at a Brazilian diplomatic mission or consulate for more than ten years.

The Portuguese requirement

For ordinary naturalization you must prove the ability to communicate in Portuguese. The usual evidence is the Celpe-Bras certificate, the official Brazilian proficiency exam run by INEP, offered twice a year in Brazil and at accredited centers abroad. The Ministry of Justice also accepts, among others, a degree or postgraduate diploma from a Brazilian institution accredited by the Ministry of Education, a school transcript showing completion of primary or secondary education in Brazil, the ENCCEJA school-equivalency certificate, a certificate of satisfactory completion of a Portuguese course for immigrants at an accredited higher-education institution, approval in the Brazilian Bar exam, or a medical degree revalidated through REVALIDA. Citizens of Portuguese-speaking countries follow a separate constitutional rule and are not subject to the test. The level required is communicative, not academic, but the Celpe-Bras calendar is fixed and missing a session can delay an application by six months, so this is usually the first thing to schedule.

Documents you will need

The document that stalls most applications is the foreign criminal record certificate. It must be recent, apostilled in the issuing country and translated in Brazil, and some countries issue it only in person or only to the applicant. Order it before anything else; by the time the rest of the file is ready, it may already have expired and need to be requested again.

Where and how to file

The application is opened online, on the Ministry of Justice's Naturalizar-se platform, where the forms are filled in and the documents uploaded. After the file passes an initial check, the applicant books an appointment at the Federal Police unit with jurisdiction over their residence to present the original documents and have biometrics collected. The Federal Police reports on the request and the Ministry of Justice and Public Security decides. There is no government fee for the application, and the status can be followed online.

Naturalization takes effect on the date the granting act is published in the Official Gazette, not at any later ceremony. The Ministry then notifies the Federal Police and the electoral authorities, and the published decision states whether the new citizen must appear before the electoral court to register.

How long it takes

There is no fixed statutory deadline that is reliably met in practice. Complete, well-organized applications filed in less congested units are commonly decided within several months; applications with missing or expired documents are returned for correction, and each round trip adds months. Across the whole cycle, from gathering the first document to receiving the certificate, a realistic planning horizon is one to two years. Most of that time is in the applicant's hands, not the government's, which is why preparation matters more than the filing itself.

Dual citizenship and what changes after approval

Brazil does not require naturalized citizens to renounce their original nationality, and the law expressly preserves the nationality of a Brazilian whom a foreign law recognizes as its own national. Whether you keep it depends on your country of origin: some states accept dual nationality freely, others restrict it for citizens by birth, and a few treat voluntary acquisition of another citizenship as grounds for loss. This has to be checked on the other side before you file, not after.

After publication you register as a voter (voting is mandatory in Brazil), obtain a Brazilian identity card and passport, and may enter public service through competitive examination. A naturalized Brazilian cannot hold a small number of offices reserved for citizens by birth, such as President, Vice-President or officer of the armed forces, and may lose Brazilian nationality in the narrow cases the Constitution allows, chiefly a judicial finding of activity harmful to the national interest.

Common reasons applications fail or stall

In practice the same problems recur. Residence counted from a tourist entry rather than from the residence authorization. A marriage celebrated abroad that was never registered in Brazil, so the one-year reduction does not apply. A foreign criminal record certificate that expired while the rest of the file was being gathered. A Celpe-Bras session missed by a week. A gap between two residence permits that resets the count. None of these are fatal, but each one costs months.

Frequently asked questions

How many years do I need to live in Brazil to apply for citizenship?

Four years of legal residence for ordinary naturalization, reduced to one year with a Brazilian child or a Brazilian spouse or partner, among other grounds. After fifteen years of uninterrupted residence, extraordinary naturalization requires only a clean record.

Does Brazil allow dual citizenship?

Yes. Brazil does not ask you to renounce your original nationality. Your country of origin may have its own rule.

Do I need to speak Portuguese?

For ordinary naturalization, yes, usually proven by the Celpe-Bras exam or by studies completed in Brazil. Extraordinary naturalization after fifteen years has no language test.

Can I apply while on a temporary residence permit?

Yes, provided the residence time has been accumulated under authorized residence. Indefinite residence is not a prerequisite, and the residence card is accepted even if expired at filing.

Is naturalization a right or a discretionary decision?

For extraordinary naturalization after fifteen years, the Federal Supreme Court has recognized a subjective right: once the requirements are met, the grant cannot be refused on discretionary grounds. Ordinary naturalization involves a wider assessment by the Ministry, but federal courts review refusals for legality and have ordered grants where the legal requirements were proven.

Do I need a lawyer to naturalize in Brazil?

No. The application can be filed by the applicant. A lawyer is useful when the residence count is uncertain, when documents from abroad are hard to obtain, when a prior application was returned, or when the applicant simply prefers to have the file reviewed before it goes in.
